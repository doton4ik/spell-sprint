I am building "Spell Sprint", a free trainer for Russian-speaking learners of English (A2–C1).
One exercise is PROOFREADING: the learner reads a short text (email, story, article, chat …) with
grammar mistakes in it and must find and fix them. The mistakes are chosen from marked places in
102 texts, and the app shows more of the mistake types the learner tends to miss.

I need DEEP RESEARCH on the real grammar errors that Russian-speaking learners make in written
English, so that the exercise trains the errors that matter most.

=== QUESTIONS ===
1. Which grammar errors are MOST FREQUENT in written English of Russian-speaking learners, by
   level (A2, B1, B2, C1)? Use learner corpora and research: e.g. REALEC (Russian Error-Annotated
   Learner English Corpus, HSE), ICLE (Russian subcorpus), Cambridge Learner Corpus / English
   Grammar Profile, EFCAMDAT, published studies on L1-Russian interference. Give numbers or
   percentages wherever the sources have them, and say which corpus they come from.
2. Compare with the app's distribution above. Which types are over-represented, which are
   under-represented (e.g. is "article-extra" really that rare, are prepositions under-trained)?
   Propose a TARGET share (percent of all marked places) for each type and level.
3. Which frequent error types are MISSING from the list? Examples to check: countable/uncountable
   beyond "information", the present perfect vs past simple, "the" with generic nouns, word order
   of adverbs (I very like), "make vs do", double negation, missing "there is", "it" as a dummy
   subject (Is raining), possessive 's, relative clauses (which/who/that), "will" in time clauses
   (when I will come), "a lot of / many", articles with names of places, "also/too" position.
   For each missing type: name, short description, 3 typical wrong → right examples, level,
   how frequent.
4. For each type (existing and new), give 5 TYPICAL wrong → right pairs exactly as Russian
   speakers write them (realistic, not textbook-made), and a one-line rule a learner can remember.
5. Which errors do learners usually NOT NOTICE when proofreading (they are hard to see), and which
   are easy to spot? This tells which ones are most worth training.

=== OUTPUT ===
